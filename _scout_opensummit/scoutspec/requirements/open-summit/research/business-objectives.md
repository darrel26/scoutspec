# Business Objectives Research

## Executive Summary
Open Summit operates as a Public Benefit Corporation (PBC) using a community-driven freemium business model to connect mountain hikers, trip organizers, and outdoor clubs. The platform provides free core summit discovery, partner matching, and trip coordination to maximize network effects while maintaining high trust. Revenue is generated via premium organizer toolkits, community membership tiers, and opt-in value-added planning and safety services.

## Monetization Model
- **Freemium Core (Free Tier)**: Free access for individual hikers to discover summits, search trips, RSVP, match with partners, and participate in trip coordination chats.
- **Organizer & Outdoor Club Subscription (SaaS)**: Monthly or annual subscriptions for certified guides, regional hiking clubs, and high-volume organizers. Includes advanced roster management, custom participant vetting questionnaires, automated safety waivers, broadcast messaging, and club analytics.
- **Value-Added Trip Services (Microtransactions / Add-ons)**: Premium features for individual trips such as offline high-resolution topographic map downloads, weather/route alert packs, automated gear checklist generators, and satellite/emergency contact synchronization.
- **Ethical Brand & Service Partnerships (Affiliate / Marketplace)**: Non-intrusive affiliate referral integrations for local mountain shuttles, gear rental co-ops, permit assistance, and eco-lodging, reinforcing the Public Benefit mission.

## Market & ICP
- **Primary ICP (Consumers)**: Day hikers, peak baggers, and mountaineers (ages 20–45) seeking reliable companions, shared trip logistics, and trail safety.
- **Secondary ICP (Buyers/Organizers)**: Grassroots hiking club leaders, university outdoor programs, independent certified mountain guides, and regional trail conservation non-profits.
- **Vertical Niche**: Outdoor adventure technology and community-driven recreational platforms.
- **Market Stage & Opportunity**: Expanding market capitalizing on user fatigue with legacy social platforms (Meetup transaction fees, unvetted Facebook groups) by offering a purpose-built, trust-first outdoor community.

## Strategic Unknowns & Questions
- **Organizer Monetization Friction**: Paywalling organizer features too early risks stalling grassroots trip creation. Options: Provide a generous free quota (e.g., 3 active trips/month free) or charge exclusively for commercial/monetized group events.
- **Platform Liability & Safety Risks**: Peer-to-peer summit trips expose the platform to safety hazards and legal liability. Options: Rely on standardized platform disclaimers and peer review, or enforce mandatory safety checklists, emergency contacts, and organizer verification.
- **Geographic Cold Start Problem**: Trip liquidity relies on dense regional clusters of hikers and summit trips. Options: Seed initial launch across high-density hiking hubs (e.g., Pacific Northwest, Colorado 14ers) via club partnerships before expanding broadly.
- **Mapping & Data Differentiation**: Competing with established mapping platforms (AllTrails, Gaia GPS) for value-added services. Options: Build lightweight native summit route overlays or integrate deeply with open mapping APIs (OpenStreetMap/OpenTopoMap) to avoid costly licensing fees.
